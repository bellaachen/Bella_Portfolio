01 / PRODUCT CONTEXTThe Hidden Complexity of Symbol Management

在 CFD Trading Platform 中，Symbol Management 看似只是後台的商品設定功能，實際上卻牽涉到交易權限、持倉風險，以及不同系統之間的狀態一致性。

當 Dealing 人員調整某個商品的交易權限時，改變的不只是管理後台的一筆設定資料，也可能直接影響正在使用交易平台的交易者。例如，這項變更可能決定他們能否建立新部位、增加曝險，或關閉既有持倉。

如果只有一個單純的 Enable / Disable 開關，其實很難完整概括這些交易情境。

例如，當某個商品因市場風險而需要限制交易時，是否應該禁止所有操作？如果使用者已經持有部位，是否還能平倉？而當設定發生變更時，正在使用平台的交易者又該如何得知？

這些問題讓 Symbol Management 在設計時，需要同時考慮交易業務、系統架構與使用者體驗。

02 / PRODUCT MODELSeparating Lifecycle and Trading Permissions

商品是否正在營運，與商品允許哪些交易操作，是兩個不同的問題。

如果將所有行為集中在單一 Enable 開關中，容易讓「商品是否上架」與「商品是否允許交易」產生混淆。因此在設計時，將 Symbol Lifecycle 與 Trading Mode 分開管理，能讓每個設定都有明確的業務責任。

Symbol Lifecycle

Symbol Lifecycle 描述商品在平台上的營運階段。

StatusDescription
Draft商品仍在準備階段，尚未正式上架
Active商品已正式上架，可依有效交易權限提供服務
Retired商品已完成退場流程，不再提供一般交易服務

這三個狀態代表商品從準備、正式上架到結束營運的生命週期。商品在 Active 期間，即使因市場風險而暫時限制交易，也不需要改變 Lifecycle，而是透過 Trading Mode 控制允許的交易行為。

在這套設計中，Retired 被定義為商品的終態。退場後仍需保留歷史訂單、成交紀錄及必要的稽核資訊，但不再直接重新啟用原有商品設定。商品轉入 Retired 前，也必須先完成必要的持倉與未結訂單處置。

Trading Mode

Trading Mode 則負責定義商品允許的交易操作。

ModeTrading Behavior
Full Trade允許符合其他限制的正常交易
Long Only限制新增或增加空頭曝險
Short Only限制新增或增加多頭曝險
Close Only只允許符合規則的減倉或平倉
No Trade禁止一般交易操作，但不必將商品從列表中隱藏

這樣的拆分，讓商品即使處於 Active，也可以因市場或風險因素被設定為 Close Only，而不需要直接改變商品的營運生命週期。

Long Only 與 Short Only 分別用來限制不同方向的新增曝險，而不是單純禁止 Buy 或 Sell 操作。因為在不同持倉模式下，相同的買賣方向可能代表建立新部位，也可能是減少既有持倉。因此，交易權限需要根據實際的持倉變化判斷，避免限制新開倉的同時，也意外阻止使用者平倉。

03 / BUSINESS LOGICUnderstanding Trading Restrictions

當 Lifecycle 與 Trading Mode 分開後，交易平台仍需要回答一個問題：

現在交易者可以對這個商品執行哪些操作？

當商品狀態、Trading Mode，以及帳戶與風險限制同時存在時，系統需要判斷交易者最終允許執行哪些操作。這個結果稱為 Effective Trading Permissions（最終生效的交易權限），由系統依照適用規則計算，並作為交易後端驗證訂單的依據，而不是管理員需要額外維護的欄位。

其中最重要的原則，是較寬鬆的設定不能意外覆蓋更嚴格的限制。

例如，某個商品雖然設定為 Full Trade，但如果交易服務因異常報價而無法安全執行訂單，後端仍應依相關安全規則限制交易。

Close Only Is More Than Disabling Buy or Sell

Close Only 是最能體現交易權限複雜度的例子。

假設使用者持有 1 Lot 的 EURUSD 多頭部位。當商品進入 Close Only 時，賣出 0.5 Lot 可能是部分平倉；但在 Netting 模式下，賣出 2 Lots 可能在關閉原本多頭後，進一步建立新的空頭曝險。

兩者雖然都是 Sell，實際產生的風險卻完全不同。

示意僅用來說明曝險判定邏輯；Hedging 模式下，交易指令與持倉關聯可能不同。

在 Hedging 與 Netting 等不同持倉模式下，訂單如何影響既有部位，也可能有所差異。因此，Close Only 不應只是停用某個交易方向，而應依照持倉模式、訂單用途及預期執行後的曝險變化，判斷操作是否符合限制。

Pending Orders、部分平倉，以及可能造成反向開倉的訂單，也必須遵循一致的規則。同樣的邏輯也適用於 Long Only 與 Short Only：限制特定方向的新增曝險，不應意外阻止使用者關閉原本持有的反向部位。

這讓交易權限不再只是前端的按鈕控制，而是交易後端必須正確執行的業務邏輯。

04 / SYSTEM CONSISTENCYFrom Configuration to Trading Platform

交易權限的另一個挑戰，是設定變更如何反映在正在使用的平台上。

假設交易者正在查看 EURUSD，此時 Dealing 人員將 Trading Mode 從 Full Trade 調整為 Close Only。

如果平台只在頁面載入時透過 API 取得商品狀態，使用者可能仍然看到可以開倉的操作介面。即使後端最終拒絕訂單，這樣的體驗仍然容易造成困惑。

讓前端及時知道狀態改變，以及確保後端始終正確執行交易限制。

Keeping the Frontend Updated

REST API 負責提供商品目前的有效交易狀態，讓平台在初次載入、切換帳戶或重新連線時取得最新資料。

當交易權限發生變更時，系統可以透過 WebSocket 發送狀態變更通知，讓已連線的客戶端及時更新。通知中應包含商品識別資訊與版本資訊，協助前端辨識狀態是否過期。

如果發生斷線、事件遺漏或版本不連續，則透過 API 重新取得有效狀態，而不是假設前端已收到所有更新。

Backend Validation Remains Essential

即使前端能即時更新，後端驗證仍然不可省略。因為從設定生效到前端收到通知之間，可能存在短暫時間差。

如果使用者在這段時間送出訂單，交易後端仍必須根據當下有效的交易限制判斷是否接受。

前端的狀態同步是為了降低使用者困惑；後端的權限驗證則是為了確保交易規則不被繞過。

Making the Status Visible

在 Back Office 中，Lifecycle 與 Trading Mode 應清楚分區，並提供 Effective Trading Status 預覽。

同時，設定儲存與設定生效需要被明確區分。若交易服務尚未採用最新版本，管理員應能辨識設定仍在套用中，而不是只看到 Save Successfully。

在 Trading Platform 中，受限制的商品也不一定需要從 Market Watch 消失。對仍持有部位的使用者而言，保留商品資訊並清楚標示 Close Only、No Trade 等狀態，通常比讓商品突然消失更容易理解。

例如 EURUSD 進入 Close Only 時：

EURUSD is currently Close Only. You can reduce or close existing positions, but opening new positions is not allowed.

如果使用者在前端狀態尚未更新時送出訂單，平台應提供明確的拒絕原因，並重新同步最新狀態。

05 / REFLECTIONClosing Thoughts

交易系統的安全性，不應完全建立在限制使用者操作之上。

以 Close Only 為例，它的目的不是單純禁止交易，而是在控制新增風險的同時，盡可能保留使用者管理既有持倉的能力。

如果不區分情境，一律禁止所有操作，雖然看似保守，卻可能讓交易者承擔更大的市場風險。

這也是 Symbol Management 值得深入設計的原因。

商品生命週期、交易權限與系統同步，看起來是不同的功能，實際上卻共同決定交易者在特定時間能夠執行什麼操作。

好的交易權限設計，需要同時站在平台方跟交易者方，思考哪些操作應該被禁止、被保留。

01 / PRODUCT CONTEXTThe Hidden Complexity of Symbol Management

In a CFD trading platform, Symbol Management may look like a routine back-office configuration feature. In practice, it affects trading permissions, position risk, and consistency across systems.

When a member of the Dealing team changes a symbol's trading permissions, more than a configuration record changes. Traders using the platform may suddenly find that they cannot open a position, increase exposure, or close an existing one.

A simple Enable / Disable switch cannot adequately describe all of these situations.

When market risk requires restrictions, should every trading action be blocked? What happens to traders who already hold positions? And how should an active user learn that a symbol's rules have changed?

These questions turn Symbol Management into a product-design problem that spans trading operations, system architecture, and user experience.

02 / PRODUCT MODELSeparating Lifecycle and Trading Permissions

Whether a symbol is offered on the platform and what trades it permits are two different questions.

Combining both concerns into one Enable switch makes it difficult to distinguish listing status from trading restrictions. Separating Symbol Lifecycle from Trading Mode gives each setting a clear responsibility.

Figure 01 · Two Independent Dimensions

Lifecycle and Trading Mode are configured independently; effective trading actions are computed from applicable restrictions.

Conceptual model: two separate administrative dimensions contribute to the final trading decision.

Symbol Lifecycle

Symbol Lifecycle describes the symbol's operational stage on the platform.

StatusDescription
DraftBeing configured; not yet available for live trading
ActiveListed on the platform; trading is subject to effective restrictions
RetiredCompleted the retirement process; no longer available for ordinary trading

These three stages cover preparation, active service, and retirement. A symbol can remain Active while trading is temporarily restricted through its Trading Mode; a separate Suspended lifecycle state is not necessary for that purpose.

Retired is a terminal state in this proposed model. Historical orders, executions, and audit records remain available. Open positions and pending orders must be appropriately handled before retirement is completed.

Trading Mode

Trading Mode defines the trading actions permitted for a symbol.

ModeTrading Behavior
Full TradeNormal trading, subject to other applicable restrictions
Long OnlyRestricts opening or increasing short exposure
Short OnlyRestricts opening or increasing long exposure
Close OnlyAllows eligible reductions or closures of existing positions
No TradeBlocks ordinary trading actions without necessarily hiding the symbol

With these concerns separated, an Active symbol can switch to Close Only when risk needs to be reduced, without changing its lifecycle.

Long Only and Short Only restrict new exposure in opposite directions; they should not be implemented as simple Buy / Sell button restrictions. Depending on the position model, a Buy or Sell order may open exposure or reduce an existing position. Validation must consider the resulting position so that a restriction on opening trades does not accidentally block an eligible close.

03 / BUSINESS LOGICUnderstanding Trading Restrictions

Once Lifecycle and Trading Mode are separated, the platform still needs to answer a practical question:

What can this trader actually do with this symbol right now?

When lifecycle status, Trading Mode, account-level restrictions, and risk controls coexist, the system must determine which actions are ultimately allowed. This computed outcome is called Effective Trading Permissions. It is used to validate orders on the trading backend—not maintained as another editable back-office field.

A less restrictive setting must not accidentally override a stricter applicable rule.

For example, a symbol may be set to Full Trade, yet the backend must still restrict execution if unreliable quotes make trading unsafe. That is an execution-safety constraint, not a reason to introduce another lifecycle status.

Close Only Is More Than Disabling Buy or Sell

Close Only illustrates why trading permissions are more complicated than button availability.

Suppose a trader holds a long EURUSD position of 1.0 lot. Under Close Only, selling 0.5 lot may partially reduce that position. In a netting account, selling 2.0 lots could close the long position and create new short exposure.

Both actions are Sell orders, but their risk outcomes are different.

A Sell action is not inherently a close; its impact depends on the existing position and execution model.

Hedging and netting models can also handle positions differently. Close Only must therefore consider the position model, the purpose of the order, and its expected effect on exposure—not merely the direction of the button pressed.

Pending orders, partial closes, and potential position reversals need consistent rules. The same principle applies to Long Only and Short Only: limiting new exposure should not inadvertently prevent eligible reductions of existing positions.

Trading permissions are therefore not merely a front-end interaction rule. They are business logic that the trading backend must enforce.

04 / SYSTEM CONSISTENCYFrom Configuration to Trading Platform

Another challenge is making configuration changes visible to people who are already trading.

Imagine a trader viewing EURUSD while a Dealing operator changes its Trading Mode from Full Trade to Close Only.

If the platform only fetches symbol status when a page loads, the trader may still see controls for opening a position. Even if the backend rejects the order, the experience is confusing.

The front end needs timely updates, while the backend must consistently enforce the rules.

Keeping the Frontend Updated

A REST API provides a snapshot of current effective trading restrictions when the platform loads, the account changes, or the client reconnects.

When permissions change, a WebSocket event can notify connected clients. Including the symbol identifier and a version allows the client to detect stale state and request a fresh snapshot.

After disconnections, missed events, or version gaps, the client should resynchronize through the API rather than assume every update was received.

Backend Validation Remains Essential

Real-time UI updates do not eliminate the need for backend validation. There can still be a short interval between a rule taking effect and a client receiving the update.

If a trader submits an order during that interval, the trading backend must evaluate it against the rules effective at the relevant validation point.

Client synchronization reduces confusion; backend validation protects the trading rules from being bypassed.

Making the Status Visible

In the Back Office, Lifecycle and Trading Mode should be clearly separated, with a preview of the effective trading status.

Saving a setting is not always the same as applying it. If trading services have not adopted the new configuration version, operators should be able to see that it is still pending rather than receive only a generic “Save Successfully” message.

Restricted symbols do not necessarily need to disappear from Market Watch. For traders with existing positions, keeping the symbol visible and clearly labeled Close Only or No Trade is often more informative.

For example, when EURUSD becomes Close Only:

EURUSD is currently Close Only. You can reduce or close existing positions, but opening new positions is not allowed.

If the backend rejects an order because permissions have changed, the platform should explain why and refresh the symbol's status.

05 / REFLECTIONClosing Thoughts

Trading safety should not rely solely on blocking user actions.

Close Only, for example, is not simply a way to stop trading. It aims to limit new exposure while preserving traders' ability to manage existing positions wherever execution can be performed safely.

Blocking every action without considering the circumstances may look conservative, but it can leave traders exposed to additional market risk.

That is why Symbol Management deserves more careful design.

Lifecycle, trading restrictions, and state synchronization may look like separate features. Together, they determine what a trader can do at a particular moment.

Good trading-permission design considers both the platform's risk responsibilities and the trader's ability to manage positions—deciding not only what to restrict, but also what should remain possible.
