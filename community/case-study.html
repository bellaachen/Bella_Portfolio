COMMUNITY PLATFORM · 0→1

A group should feel like home the moment you walk in.

I planned a Groups feature from scratch inside an app that combines messaging, games and membership. Users can create and join groups, post, comment, react and share.

Product Manager · 0→1 featureiOS · Android · Interactive prototype

THE PROBLEM

Friends and chat, but nowhere to gather.

The app already had friends and chat. Chat works for real-time conversation, but not for people with a shared interest who want to build up content over time. Users had no place to return to and see what each other were up to.

Operations needed it too. Event news needed a way to spread: during a yearly event, each organiser shares the event post into their own group to bring members in.

We modelled it on Facebook Groups because users already know how it works, so there is little to learn. The real work was deciding, within a fixed schedule, what to copy as is, what to adapt for our users, and what to leave for later phases.

GOALS

A sense of belonging, and interactions that cost as little as possible.

Every spec opened with its purpose, for users and for the business, so design and engineering had shared ground for trade-offs.

DECISION 1

The lobby's first screen: immersion versus posting.

Two goals in the lobby spec pull against each other. The lobby should be "immersive and welcoming", and the post entry should be "quiet, out of the way, with no scrolling back to the top to post". A bigger cover feels more like home, but it pushes content and the post entry further down.

Cover image: go big

Design and I compared three sizes:

OptionRatioCover share of first screenFirst post readable without scrolling
Standard16:926%Yes
Larger4:335%Barely
Immersive (shipped)Full-bleed, group name over the image56%No

Shares measured on a 390 × 844 phone screen in the rebuilt prototype.

We chose immersive. The cover is where an admin can best express the group's personality: pick a preset or upload their own photo. The bigger it is, the more that one image stands for the whole group, which speaks directly to the "feel at home" goal. When you scroll, the Discussion, About, Members and Photos tabs stick to the top, so switching tabs never means scrolling back.

Post entry: top or floating

With a bigger cover, where the post entry sits becomes the key question.

OptionProsCons
Composer at the top (shipped)Matches mainstream social apps, nothing to learn; never covers contentFurther away under a bigger cover; posting mid-scroll means scrolling back up
Floating + buttonPost at any point while browsingCovers a corner of posts; new users may not read + as "post"
Top plus floating (my proposal)New users use the top composer; browsers use the floating buttonOne more element on screen; needs a rule for when it appears

I argued for adding the floating entry. Content in a group feeds itself: people often decide to post after reading someone else's. If they first have to scroll back to the top, the idea is easily lost.

The shipped version kept only the top composer. The prototype keeps my proposal: the composer sits at the top, and the floating button appears only after you scroll past it, so it stays out of the way. Given the chance, I would compare how often people post straight from browsing the lobby, and let that decide whether to add the floating entry.

DECISION 2

Make every interaction lighter.

Effort runs from posting to commenting to reacting. My approach was to make the lightest interaction the easiest to do, then strip friction out of commenting and sharing.

DECISION 3

Create first, decorate after.

Creating a group takes only a name and a category. The picture, cover and description come after, behind "Start setting up" or "Do it later". Admins are not stopped by a long form at step one, and "Do it later" still lands them in the lobby with the invite sheet open, so a new group has people from the start.

Admin creates a group
01Name and categoryUp to 15 characters; pick one of 10 categories.

05DescriptionUp to 5,000 characters, then the lobby opens with invites.

Joining depends on a review setting that admins can turn on for public and private groups alike; it is on by default for private groups and off for public ones. Phase 1 had no entry questions, and applicants could not cancel a request; they could join and leave instead.

Member joins a group

No review

Tap "Join group" and you are a member. The default for public groups, where anyone can read posts before joining.

Review on

Tap "Request to join" and wait in the review list. Admins approve or reject one by one, or all at once. The default for private groups, where non-members only see the About tab.

Phase 1 had no privacy setting at creation; new groups were public. With five preset pictures and one cover style in several colours, admins could finish without preparing anything, or upload their own photos for more personality and swap them with another tap.

SPEC TO BUILD

Write the spec so engineering can build from it.

What gets lost when you copy a mature product is the rules behind the screens. Each spec spelled out copy, limits, error handling and the unhappy paths, so iOS, Android and the backend behaved the same.

Group names

Up to 15 characters, no half- or full-width spaces at either end; spaces in the middle are fine. The client checks first and the backend checks again. Duplicate names return an error code and the field shows "Sorry, that name is taken".

Length limits

Posts, comments and descriptions all allow 5,000 characters. Typing and pasting are defined separately; overflow is cut with a notice. Descriptions get 5,000 on purpose so admins can use them as a group wiki.

Uploading a post

The lobby shows an "Uploading post…" bar. Leaving prompts the user to wait; force-quitting the app cancels the upload; a failed upload can be reopened from the bar.

Shared links

A deleted post, a disbanded group and a blocked viewer each lead to their own page, with the back path defined for each.

Phased release

PhaseScope
Phase 1Create group, lobby, posting, comments and replies, likes and reactions, sharing post links, joining and leaving; new groups are public
Phase 2Shares as post objects; recommended posts and groups
Phase 3 and laterCamera and stickers in comments, comment sorting (top / newest), reaction lists

Data

With the data team I defined four groups of metrics: overall group health (groups, members, impressions), post activity (posts, comments, reactions, shares, link clicks), member profiles, and, in phase 2, recommended content.

REFLECTION

Copy what users know; decide what not to build.

Looking back, the biggest value of modelling a mature product was saving users the cost of learning it. My judgement went into deciding what this version would leave out. If I did it again, I would explore these first:

Entry questions

Admins could already review requests in phase 1, but could not yet ask entry questions to find people who really want to join rather than advertise.

Text on shares

Left out of phase 1 for speed; after launch, sharing behaviour can show whether to add it.
