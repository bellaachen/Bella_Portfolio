WHY A REBUILD

What would this product look like if I built it today?

I first planned this tool in 2018. I wanted to see what it would look like if I built it again with today's data and thinking. The rebuild keeps the original goal, and fills in the input fields and calculation details I could no longer recover from back then. The decisions below are all about this rebuild.

THE PROBLEM

The hard part is understanding the reward rules.

Plenty of reward calculators exist: enter an amount and a reward rate, and they tell you how much you get back. A phone calculator can do that. The harder part is that people often don't know which rate actually applies to them.

One card can have channel bonuses, monthly caps, registration requirements, new-customer offers and end dates. That information is scattered across offer pages, and finding it all and doing the maths yourself is not easy.

Channel bonuses

3% on online shopping, 1% on everything else.

Caps

The online bonus is capped at NT$150 a month; beyond that only the base rate applies.

Thresholds and tasks

You may need to register, link a specific wallet, switch plans or hit a monthly spend.

New customers only

The headline rate may apply only to new customers.

Offer periods

Offers can change every quarter, so the data needs constant updating.

A 2026 example: for the same card, one comparison site says "11% on online shopping", while another breaks it down as "1% base + 2% online (capped at NT$150) + 10% for new customers". An existing customer actually gets 3%.

GOALS

What I wanted it to do.

Fill in your monthly spending once, and see how the cards rank, the estimated reward, and how each number was worked out. I set four principles for the design:

Use their own spending

Rank by what each person spends, not by the headline "up to X%".

Never over-count unclear data

When the data is unclear, leave it out and say why. Overstate a reward once and people stop trusting the tool.

Every number is traceable

Including the rate for each category and whether a cap was hit.

The user decides on conditions

Registering, linking or switching plans takes time, and not everyone wants to.

DECISION 1

Estimate from a month of spending.

I designed the input as "roughly how much you spend in each category each month", rather than a single purchase amount.

There are two reasons. Many caps and thresholds are counted per month, so one purchase can't tell you whether the month goes over a cap. And each card suits different people: someone who shops online a lot and someone who travels often may get different recommendations.

8 spending categories

General, dining, online shopping, mobile wallets, transport, subscriptions, overseas and insurance, based on the channels banks most often reward.

Spending profiles

Eats out, shops online, travels, commutes and pays premiums. People pick the closest profile and adjust, instead of filling eight fields from zero.

The categories are a trade-off. "Mobile wallets" is a way to pay rather than a type of spending, and whether a shop counts as one of the "top 100 merchants" depends on the shop. Too fine, and people can't fill it in; too coarse, and channel bonuses get missed. This version favours easy input and explains what may be missed in the breakdown.

DECISION 2

Separate the base reward from the reward after conditions.

Every card gets two results. By default the tool shows what you get without doing anything. Only when you tick "Include up-to rewards" does it add bonuses that need registration, a linked wallet, a plan switch or a task.

Take NT$8,000 on dining, NT$5,000 online and NT$3,000 general spending a month. The top three differ between the two modes:

The top card changes, and the amount nearly doubles. Showing only one number could make some people think it is what they are sure to get.

I made "without doing anything" the default because registrations and tasks are easy to forget. Show what people will get for sure first, then what they could get with the conditions met, and let them decide whether it is worth it.

DECISION 3

Break offers into rules that can be calculated.

A card's offer isn't stored as a line of marketing copy. It is broken into "spending category × reward rule". Adding a card or updating an offer means updating data, not rewriting the calculation.

DECISION 4

Show where every number comes from.

The result is more than a ranking. Each card opens to show every category's amount, rate, reward and conditions, with five labels:

Conditions apply

The reward needs registration, linking or a task.

Cap reached

The bonus is used up; more spending adds no bonus.

Below threshold

This month's spending hasn't reached the bonus threshold.

Not stated

The data says nothing about this category, so it isn't counted.

To be confirmed

Sources disagree, or the number is inferred.

With conditions left off, the breakdown still hints "Up to X% with conditions", so people can see what they might gain without switching modes.

I also added a two-card split: spending is shared between two cards by category to find the higher combined reward. For someone who eats out a lot, general and dining on Cash Back Premier and online on Diamond comes to NT$284 a month, only NT$24 more than Diamond alone. Showing the difference lets people judge whether a second card is worth it.

EDGE CASES

Edge cases and data quality.

Most of the rebuild went into organising data. Many comparison sites only say "up to X%" and don't always list the base rate, the cap or the offer period.

CaseHow it is handled
Bonus over the capOnly the base rate counts above the cap; marked "Cap reached"

Monthly threshold not metNo bonus; shows current spending

Cap with no stated periodTreated as monthly
Points, P coins, LINE POINTSValued at 1 point = NT$1

Sources can also contradict each other. For SinoPac's Dual Currency card, a single page shows 13%, 7%/8% and 1%/2%. In those cases I use the more clearly broken-down data and note it on the card. Numbers not stated in a source but inferred from it are marked "Inferred".

Some cards are too complex to calculate accurately in this version, so they are left out of the ranking and listed below it with the reason: miles cards (the reward isn't cash), travel cards that need a destination country, and cards whose rewards depend on the plan you switch to.

REFLECTION

Most of this product is organising data and checking rules.

The rebuild showed me again how much of this product is about organising data and confirming rules. The same card can be described very differently on two comparison sites: one only says "up to X%", the other breaks out the base rate, bonus and cap. Look only at the headline numbers and the recommendation can come out completely different.

If I kept going, these come first:

Fill spending from invoices

Instead of asking people to estimate, work out their category mix from past purchase records.

Overseas by country

Bonuses differ between Japan, Korea and Thailand; today there is one "overseas" category.

Plan-switching cards

Compare each plan, then suggest which one fits the user's spending.

More categories

Such as food delivery, fuel and eTag.

Offer expiry reminders

Give each rule a valid period, and flag it for updating before it ends.
