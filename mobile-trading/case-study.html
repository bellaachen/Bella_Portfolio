MOBILE FINTECH · WEB TO NATIVE

Moving a trading platform onto the phone, without moving its problems.

I was a product manager on a trading platform as it went from web-only to native iOS and Android apps, launched in 25 countries. I wrote the specs for onboarding and KYC, the order ticket, copy trading and the system states around them. This prototype rebuilds those flows from my specs, with fictional data.

THE PROBLEM

A web platform squeezed onto a phone.

The web platform had grown one screen at a time. Many trading rules lived in the backend or in people's heads, and the web could get away with it: there was room for long forms, tooltips and a support chat next to everything.

On a phone, every unclear rule turns into an error the user can't act on. And one app had to serve users under several regulators, each with its own onboarding questions, documents and terms. Moving screens across one by one would have moved the problems with them.

GOALS

Three things every flow had to do.

Get new users to a live account without getting lost

Onboarding and KYC are long. Users should always know where they are, what is left, and what happens next, even after leaving halfway.

Show the trading rule before the order is sent

If the market is closed, the order type isn't allowed or margin is short, the order ticket should say so in place, in the same words the backend uses.

Give every state a screen

Loading, empty, error, offline, maintenance, a closed market, a restricted region. These are where trust is won or lost, so they were specced, not left to engineering to improvise.

DECISION 1

KYC: show progress, not a form.

A new user starts on a demo account. Instead of news and promotions, the first thing on their home screen is their verification progress, with one clear next action. Each application status has its own card, so the home screen always answers "what do I do now?"

StatusWhat the user seesNext action
Not startedThree steps: account details, terms, documentsContinue
In progressCompleted steps ticked, the rest marked incompleteContinue from the first unfinished step
Under reviewAll steps complete, "Processing your application"None; no way to submit twice
ResubmitWhat failed and that documents are needed againContinue Verification, straight to the document step
Re-verifying"We're checking your updated details"None
RejectedThe review is complete and the application can't proceedView Details, with the reason and a support contact

The country of residence decides the rules

Which regulator a user falls under depends on their country of residence, and that decides the questions, documents and terms they see. So the country is asked first, unsupported countries are stopped at that step rather than halfway through, and the app takes the regulator from the backend instead of hard-coding country rules into a release.

If a user changes their country later and it moves them to a different regulator, a "Confirm Regulation" sheet explains the change, and the account type, funding currency and leverage they chose have to be picked again. If their answers show the product isn't suitable for them, the flow stops with a plain explanation instead of a generic error.

DECISION 2

Put the trading rules into the order ticket.

An order can be rejected for many reasons. I wrote the checks as an ordered list, so the user always sees the reason that matters most first, worded exactly as the backend would reject it. The Place Orders button stays disabled until every check passes.

CheckExample message
1. Market hours"Market is closed. Orders can only be placed or modified during trading hours." plus a countdown to the open
2. Order type"Stop orders are not acceptable for this instrument"
3. Trade direction"Only Buy Orders Are Accepted For This Instrument", or "New Positions Are Not Accepted" for close-only instruments
4. Prices and sizeA buy limit must be below the ask; a stop loss must be on the losing side; a minimum number of units
5. Margin"Insufficient free margin" with the amount required and the amount available

Before sending, the ticket shows the estimated trade value, margin and leverage. Each stop-loss type (standard, top-of-book or guaranteed) previews its distance in points, the loss in dollars and any premium, so the cost of protection is visible before it is chosen.

After sending, the status screen separates "request submitted" from "filled", "pending" and "rejected". A rejection says plainly that no order was placed, so nobody wonders whether to try again.

DECISION 3

Copy trading: settings in one place, money in another.

Copy trading had a page for finding signals but nowhere to manage the ones you were already copying. I put "My Copy Trading" next to "Signals" in the Copy Trading tab, so discovering and managing live in the same place. Portfolio only gets a summary card that links there.

Each copy is a card with its status (copying, paused or ended), its fees and six numbers: stop-loss, trade size, margin call level, accumulated P&L, high-water mark and next billing day. The actions are split by how much they risk:

Settings behind one icon

Stop-loss, copy type and multiplier open from a single settings icon on the card. Each change applies to new copied trades only, and a failed save keeps the previous setting.

Money and endings behind ⋮

Removing funds and stopping a copy are separate from settings. Stopping explains that all open positions will be closed and can't be undone, and asks again.

Two copy types and a multiplier

The original product copied by balance ratio ("Smart Scaling"). I added "Exact Copy", which copies the provider's exact lot size, and planned a multiplier on top of either.

Validation that explains itself

Empty values, too many decimals and out-of-range numbers each get their own message. A high multiplier on a small account shows a margin-call warning without blocking the user.

SPEC TO BUILD

The states nobody draws, written down.

Most of my specs were about what happens when things go wrong. These are the rules that made the app predictable across iOS, Android and the backend:

Maintenance and forced updates

Both are switched on remotely without a release. Neither can be dismissed. The maintenance screen says when service returns and that open positions and stop-losses still apply.

Offline and stale prices

When the connection drops, the screen stays, prices turn grey and a banner says they may be outdated, so an old price is never mistaken for a live one.

Restricted regions

Users outside supported regions see why live trading is unavailable and continue in demo only, confirmed with a one-second press so the message is read, not tapped away.

Demo, live and copy accounts

The account type is always visible. Switching to live shows the account, regulator and equity first. A copy account is close-only: positions can be closed or modified, but new orders are blocked with a reason.

Local conventions

Up and down colours are a setting: green-up by default, red-up for Taiwan. The app also had to support right-to-left Arabic.

Empty, loading and error

Three different screens, never one. Empty explains how data will appear; loading uses skeletons, not a full-page spinner; errors give the reason, a retry and the last update time.

REFLECTION

The spec is the product's memory.

Rebuilding these flows showed me how much of my spec lived in design files rather than in writing. The team worked from the designs day to day, so it shipped, but the rules for the unhappy paths were easy to lose once people moved on. If I did it again, I would:

Write acceptance criteria for every state

Not only the happy path: empty, error, offline and rejected states in the ticket itself, so anyone reading it later knows what the app should do.

Test a review step for first-time live orders

The live app sends orders straight from the ticket. A short review sheet could help first-time live traders; I would test it with them before deciding.
